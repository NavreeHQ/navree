Components carry no outer margin. Space between them comes from four primitives: `Stack` puts items one above the other, `Row` side by side, `Grid` in equal columns, and `Container` holds content to a width. Gaps are in units of 4 px and snap to the scale (0 to 6, 8, 10, 12, 16), because the content security policy allows no `style` attribute.

```rust
use navree::ui::*;

Stack::gap(3).children((
    Heading::h3("Sutlej Supply renewal"),
    Text::muted("Proposal · closes 30 November"),
    Row::gap(2).children((Button::primary("Mark won"), Button::outline("Edit"))),
))
```

## A grid

`Grid::cols(n)` makes 1 to 6 equal columns from 768 px up, and one column on phones. Its gap is 16 px unless you set it. The grid previews here are drawn 820 px wide and scaled to the column, so they show the wide layout; open one on its own and narrow the window to see a phone's.

<UiPreview example="layout/grid" />

`Grid::fit(min)` is the grid for a row of tiles: as many columns as fit, each at least `min` rem wide (8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 20 or 24; another number takes the nearest), sharing the width equally. It runs from one column on a phone to six on a wide screen with no breakpoint to choose, so a row of [Stat](/docs/components/stat/) tiles wraps by the room it has.

```rust
use navree::ui::*;

Grid::fit(12).gap(3).children((
    Stat::new("Steps", "9,412"),
    Stat::new("Sleep", "7 h 20 min"),
    Stat::new("Resting heart rate", "58 bpm"),
))
```

### Columns in a ratio

`Grid::ratio([7, 5])` makes columns of unequal width, in the ratio of the parts: a wide column of trends beside a narrower one of notes, or `[3, 2]`, or `[1, 2, 1]`. Two to four columns, each part 1 to 12. From 768 px up the columns stand side by side; on a phone they stack in the order given.

```rust
use navree::ui::*;

Grid::ratio([7, 5]).gap(4).children((
    Card::new().title("Trends").body(Text::new("The last 30 nights.")),
    Card::new().title("Notes").body(Text::new("What changed this week.")),
))
```

## On a phone

Below 768 px a grid is one column, whatever `Grid::cols` says, and a component that spans columns takes the whole width. The cards of the spanning example above, drawn at 390 px, the width of a phone:

<UiPreview example="layout/span-phone" />

The items stay in the order you wrote them, so put the main content first and the aside after it. A [SplitPane](/docs/components/split-pane/) stacks its panes below the width you choose, and the [app shell](/docs/ui/app-shell/#on-a-phone) turns its sidebar into a sheet.

## Common page layouts

Most pages are one of these. Each is a few of the primitives above, and the previews on this page show them.

- **A main column and an aside.** `Grid::cols(3)` with `.span(2)` on the main component, as in the spanning example. A record with its activity beside it, or a report with its notes.
- **A row of figures over the content.** `Grid::cols(4)` of [Stat](/docs/components/stat/)s above a [Table](/docs/components/table/) or a [DataTable](/docs/ui/data-table/), as in the grid example.
- **Two panes.** A list on the left and the open item on the right, in a [SplitPane](/docs/components/split-pane/) whose panes the reader resizes. On a phone the panes stack: `.stack_below(SplitWidth::Md)` sets where.

  <UiPreview example="split-pane/stacked" />

- **A sidebar and a page.** Every page of an app inside the [app shell](/docs/ui/app-shell/), with the sidebar docked on a desktop and off-canvas on a phone.
- **A list with a drawer.** One record opened over its list in a [Sheet](/docs/components/sheet/), so the reader keeps their place.
- **A narrow form.** A `Container` at `ContainerSize::Sm`, centred, as in the container example.
