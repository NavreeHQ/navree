`SortableGrid` draws tiles in a grid that people arrange themselves: the tiles of a dashboard, of a home-screen widget, of a launcher. A tile moves with a mouse, with a finger or with the keyboard alone, and its remove button takes it away. The page changes at once, and half a second after the last change the grid posts the whole order to your server, which saves it.

```rust
use navree::ui::*;

SortableGrid::new("tiles", "Simran's widget")
    .post("/widgets/7/tiles")
    .columns(2)
    .tile(GridTile::new("health", "Health score").fixed())
    .tile(GridTile::new("recovery", "Recovery"))
    .tile(GridTile::new("sleep", "Sleep"))
```

Without `.post(...)` the tiles are drawn in their grid with no handles, no buttons and no script.

## When to use it

Use it when people choose which tiles they see and in what order, and the order is one list: the blocks of a widget, the cards of a personal dashboard. For work that moves between columns, or a list that is long and loads by pages, use [Sortable](/docs/components/sortable/), which posts each move on its own and checks it against the lists.

## Columns

`.columns(n)` draws exactly `n` columns, 1 to 6, at every width. `.fit(GridFit::Narrow)`, `Medium` (the default) or `Wide` draws as many columns as fit, each tile at least 8, 12 or 16 rem wide, so a phone shows one or two and a wide screen more.

## Saving the order

The grid holds one form. A moment after the last change (500 ms, or `.autosave_ms(ms)`), it writes every tile's key into the form as an `order` field, in the order the page shows them, adds a `remove` field for each tile taken away, and submits it through the page's own forms. So the post carries the session's token, quick changes are one post, a change made while a post is on its way is sent after it with the latest order, and the answer's ops apply: draw a preview again, update a count. Each change also fires a `ui:grid-change` event on the grid, with the order and the tile taken away.

A failed save leaves the page as it is and says so with a toast. The next change posts the whole order again, so nothing is lost and nothing needs putting back.

Read the post with `GridOrder` and put it together with the tiles you hold with `arrange`:

```rust
use navree::ui::*;

fn save(order: GridOrder, now: &[String]) -> Reply {
    let arranged = order.clone().keep(["health"]).arrange(now);
    if arranged.changed {
        // Save arranged.order in one write.
    }
    order.done("/widgets/7")
}
```

`arrange` trusts nothing in the post. The tiles come in the order posted; a key you no longer hold is passed over, and a tile the page didn't know of (added in another window) keeps its place at the end, so a page drawn earlier never loses one. Only the tiles named in `remove` go, never one you `keep`.

## Moving with the keys

Each tile has a handle, a button named "Move" and the tile's name. Press **Space** or **Enter** to pick the tile up, the **arrow keys** to move it (left and right one place, as the page reads; up and down a row), **Home** and **End** to go to the ends, **Space** or **Enter** to drop it, and **Escape** to put it back. Leaving the handle with **Tab** puts it back too.

Every step is said: "Picked up Recovery. Position 2 of 4." then "Position 3 of 4." then "Dropped Recovery at position 3 of 4." Taking a tile away says "Removed Sleep. 3 left." and moves the focus to the next tile. Change the words with `SortableGridLabels`.

## With JavaScript off

Each tile shows two buttons, **Move earlier** and **Move later**, beside its remove button. They post the same form with `up` or `down` naming the tile, and `arrange` moves it one place. Answer with `GridOrder::done(page)` and the browser goes back to the page, the tiles in their new order.

## Keyboard and accessibility

<UiA11y>

- The grid is a group named by its label, and its tiles are an ordered list, so a screen reader reads their number and order.
- Each handle is a real `button`, at least 28 by 28 pixels and 36 on a touch screen, described by the instructions above.
- Every step of a move is said in the grid's own live region, and the focus stays on the handle of the tile that moves.
- Moving never relies on dragging: the keys and, with JavaScript off, the move buttons do everything a drag does.
- On touch, the handle drags at once and a press held still on a tile drags it; a swipe still scrolls the page.
- With reduced motion, tiles jump to their places instead of sliding.

</UiA11y>
