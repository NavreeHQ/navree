`ColorStrip` shows a sequence at a glance: the zone every heart-rate reading of a run fell in, the stage every half hour of a night was in, the state of every check of a service. Each cell is one reading, all cells are equal in width, and the colour says which category it belongs to. The legend names the categories, and `.legend_shares()` adds each one's share of the cells.

Cells are equal in width, so the strip is proportional to the number of readings and not to time. Say so beside it when readings are not evenly spaced.

## Cells by category

A cell has a label (a time, a number) and a category. Declare the categories to choose their colours and the order of the legend; a category that was not declared takes the next categorical colour.

```rust
use navree::ui::charts::*;
use navree::ui::*;

ColorStrip::new("Sleep stages, every 30 minutes")
    .category("Awake", Ink::slot(4))
    .category("REM", Ink::tone(Tone::named("cyan")))
    .category("Core", Ink::slot(3))
    .category("Deep", Ink::slot(7))
    .cell("11:00 pm", "Awake")
    .cell("11:30 pm", "Core")
    .cell("12:00 am", "Deep")
```

## Readings by band

A reading is coloured by the band of a [`ColorScale`](/docs/components/progress/#painted) its value falls in. Name each band with `label`: the legend and the table use the names. The table keeps the reading as well as its band.

```rust
use navree::ui::charts::*;
use navree::ui::*;

ColorStrip::new("Heart rate zones")
    .colors(
        ColorScale::new(Ink::tone(Tone::named("slate")))
            .label("Rest")
            .from(100, Ink::tone(Tone::named("cyan")))
            .label("Fat burn")
            .from(130, Ink::tone(Tone::Warning))
            .label("Cardio"),
    )
    .format(NumberFormat::number().suffix(" bpm"))
    .value_name("Heart rate")
    .reading("6:00 pm", 74)
    .reading("6:01 pm", 112)
    .reading("6:02 pm", 141)
```

A missing reading is a cell in the quiet colour, named "No value".

## Many cells

A strip draws at most 600 cells. A longer one draws every k-th cell and says so; the table lists the cells drawn. `.height(px)` sets the thickness, 4 to 32 (8 by default).

## Motion

`.motion(Motion::new())` fades the cells in from the start, one after another. However many cells there are, the sweep takes about a second. The cells are drawn finished, so a reader who asks for less motion sees the final state.

## Loading, failed and empty

The states work as on a [line chart](/docs/components/line-chart/#loading-failed-and-empty). A strip with no cell is empty.

## The table

The table has a row for each cell: its label, its category and, for readings, the value. `.cell_name("Half hour")` and `.value_name("Heart rate")` name the columns. See [The table](/docs/components/line-chart/#the-table).

## Keyboard and accessibility

<UiA11y>

| Key | Does |
|---|---|
| `Tab` | Moves into the chart, which is one tab stop. |
| `←`, `→`, `↑`, `↓` | Moves to the previous or the next cell. |
| `Home`, `End` | Moves to the first or the last cell. |
| `Page Up`, `Page Down` | Moves a tenth of the way back or on, and at least one cell. |
| `Esc` | Hides the tooltip. |

- The chart is a group named by its title and described by its summary: how many readings, in how many categories, and the most common one.
- A move by key marks the cell, dims the others and is announced politely: its label, its category and its reading. With a pointer the tooltip says the same, floating above the cell.
- The legend names every category, and a status tone adds an icon to its entry; the table twin names every cell's category, so colour is never alone.

</UiA11y>
