`ChartSkeleton` holds the place of a chart that hasn't been drawn yet, in its shape, so a section that loads late doesn't jump when the chart arrives. It pulses, and holds still when the reader asks for less motion. A [Skeleton](/docs/components/skeleton/) is a line or a block; a chart skeleton is the ring, the line or the grid.

```rust
use navree::ui::*;

ChartSkeleton::ring(120).label("Loading recovery")
```

## Shapes

| Shape | Constructor | Holds the place of |
|---|---|---|
| Ring | `ChartSkeleton::ring(size)` | a [ring](/docs/components/ring/), 48 to 320 px |
| Gauge | `ChartSkeleton::gauge(size)` | a [gauge](/docs/components/gauge/) |
| Line | `ChartSkeleton::line(height)` | a line or area chart, the width of its place |
| Bars | `ChartSkeleton::bars(count, height)` | a bar chart of up to 48 bars |
| Heat map | `ChartSkeleton::heat_map(weeks)` | a [calendar heat map](/docs/components/calendar-heatmap/) of 4 to 53 weeks |
| Matrix | `ChartSkeleton::matrix(size)` | a [matrix heat map](/docs/components/matrix-heatmap/), its lower triangle |
| Meter | `ChartSkeleton::meter()` | a [meter](/docs/components/meter/) and its legend |

A size outside its range is clamped, so a number from outside can't draw a huge picture.

## In a region that loads late

A chart's own `loading()` state keeps the chart's height. A skeleton is for the place where the chart isn't there at all: the first paint of a lazy [Region](/docs/ui/regions/). Give the region the skeleton as its children; the region's answer replaces it, and a region that fails hides it.

```rust
use navree::ui::*;

Region::new("heart-rate")
    .src("/fragments/heart-rate")
    .lazy()
    .children(ChartSkeleton::line(160).label("Loading heart rate"))
```

## Keyboard and accessibility

<UiA11y>

- A skeleton is one status that says what is loading ("Loading heart rate"); the shapes are hidden from assistive technology. Name it, or it says "Loading".
- The pulse stops when the reader asks for less motion.
- It draws in the toolkit's line colours, visible against the page in both light and dark.

</UiA11y>
