`Heading` writes `<h1>` to `<h6>`; its look follows its level. `Text` writes a paragraph, or a `<span>` with `.inline()`. The body text is 14 px Geist; secondary text is 13 px.

```rust
use navree::ui::*;

Stack::gap(1).children((
    Heading::h3("Quarterly review"),
    Text::muted("Pipeline, wins and losses."),
))
```

## Headings

Pick the level from the page's outline, not from the size you want. In the [app shell](/docs/ui/app-shell/) the page title is the `<h1>`.

<UiPreview example="typography/headings" />

`Heading::h5` and `Heading::h6` are for the small headings inside a card or a panel. They link the parts stylesheet, which sizes them.

```rust
Heading::h5("Billing address")
```

## Text

<UiPreview example="typography/text" />

`Text::code` writes `<code>` in the system monospace font, which costs no download.

## Tones

`.tone(Tone)` colours the words of a `Text` by what they mean: a balance at its limit in the danger colour, a paid one in green. It sets the `data-tone` a [Badge](/docs/components/badge/) reads, and the stylesheet draws it as the colour of the text and nothing else: no pill and no tint. `Tone` has five built-in values (`Neutral`, which writes nothing, `Info`, `Success`, `Warning` and `Danger`), and `Tone::named("violet")` picks one of the extra colours the stylesheet ships. A tone works on `Text::muted`, on `Text::code` and on `.inline()` text, such as a figure in a table cell, and it wins over the colour of the look. A text with no tone writes no `data-tone` and keeps the colour of its look.

<UiPreview example="typography/tones" />

```rust
Text::new("₹1,250.00 over the limit").inline().tone(Tone::Danger)
```

The colour never carries the meaning alone: say it in the words ("over the limit"). Use a [Badge](/docs/components/badge/) for a label or a status, and a toned `Text` for a figure or a phrase inside a sentence or a cell.
