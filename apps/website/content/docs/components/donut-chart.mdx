`Donut` splits one whole into parts. Each part is a slice, drawn clockwise from twelve o'clock in the order you give them. The total sits in the middle, and a legend beside the ring names each part with its value and its share, so a reader needs no hover to read it.

```rust
use navree::ui::charts::*;
use navree::ui::*;

Donut::new("Delivery")
    .part("Delivered", 9_412)
    .part_tone("Bounced", 188, Tone::Danger)
    .part("Deferred", 40)
    .part("Rejected", 12)
```

`.parts([("Desktop", 5_200), ("Phone", 3_800)])` adds parts from name and value pairs. `.part_tone(name, value, tone)` colors a part by what it means, and the legend adds the tone's icon.

## When to use it

Use a donut for a few parts of one whole, read at a glance: how messages were delivered, which plans people are on. Reading a donut is reading angles, and people judge angles less well than lengths. When the parts are close in size, or there are more than a handful, use ranked [horizontal bars](/docs/components/bar-chart/#horizontal-bars). To show one share, use a [Stat](/docs/components/stat/) or a [Progress](/docs/components/progress/). For parts over time, use a stacked [area](/docs/components/area-chart/) or stacked [bars](/docs/components/bar-chart/).

## A pie

`.pie()` draws the whole disc: no hole, and no total in the middle. The legend and the summary still give the total.

<UiPreview example="donut-chart/pie" />

## Your own words in the middle

The middle says the total by default, in the chart's number format. `.center(value, label)` says something else: a share, a headline figure. It is for the eye only, and the summary still carries the total.

<UiPreview example="donut-chart/center" />

`.size(px)` sets the width and height of the ring (96 to 320, and 168 by default).

## Parts that fold into Other

A donut draws at most six parts. `.max_parts(n)` changes that (2 to 8). When more parts have a value, the largest `n - 1` keep their slices, in the order you gave them, and the rest are added up into one called Other, drawn in gray. The table lists the parts as folded.

Parts at zero, with no value, or below zero draw nothing and stay in the table. Colors follow the order of the parts as given, before any fold, and a part with a tone takes no color of its own. A donut with no part above zero is empty.

## Formats and languages

`.format(NumberFormat)` writes the values, and the shares are always written as percentages in the chart's locale. `.locale("de")` writes them as `97,5 %`. See [Formats and languages](/docs/components/line-chart/#formats-and-languages) on the line chart page for the formats, the locales and the words a chart writes.

## Loading, failed and empty

The states work as on a [line chart](/docs/components/line-chart/#loading-failed-and-empty), and keep the height of the ring.

<UiPreview example="donut-chart/states" />

## The table

The table lists each part with its value and its share. `.table(TableMode)` says where it sits: behind a "Show table" switch (the default), shown, or for screen readers only. See [The table](/docs/components/line-chart/#the-table).

## Keyboard and accessibility

<UiA11y>

| Key | Does |
|---|---|
| `Tab` | Moves into the chart, which is one tab stop. |
| `←`, `→`, `↑`, `↓` | Moves to the previous or the next part. |
| `Home`, `End` | Moves to the first or the last part. |
| `Page Up`, `Page Down` | Moves a tenth of the way back or on, and at least one part. |
| `Esc` | Hides the tooltip. |

- The chart is a group named by its title and described by its summary: how many parts, the total, and the largest part with its share.
- A move by key marks the slice, dims the others, and is announced politely: the part's name, its value and its share. With a pointer the tooltip says the same.
- The legend is a list that names every part, its value and its share. The total in the middle is hidden from assistive technology because the summary says it.
- The table twin lists every part, including those that draw nothing.
- A slice is told apart by the legend, never by color alone. A status tone adds an icon to its legend entry, and each slice has a thin gap around it.

</UiA11y>

## Data rules

- A part's value is a finite number. NaN and infinity are read as no value: the slice is left out and the table shows a dash.
- Only positive values draw. Their sum is the total, and a share is a part of it.
- At most six parts, and then [Other](#parts-that-fold-into-other).
- The summary and the legend are written from the folded parts, and the table too.
